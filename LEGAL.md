# AYTHER Legal and Content Policy

Last updated: August 24, 2026

This document describes the legal boundaries and content rules of the public
AYTHER project. It applies to official AYTHER repositories, documentation,
examples, releases, and community spaces maintained by the project.

It is a project policy, not legal advice and not a declaration that every use of
emulation, game content, or enhancement technology is lawful in every
jurisdiction. Laws and license terms differ by country, platform, game, and use
case. Users, contributors, pack authors, and distributors are responsible for
evaluating their own activities and obtaining any permissions they require.

For legal or intellectual-property questions concerning the AYTHER project,
contact [david.lazarte@gmail.com](mailto:david.lazarte@gmail.com).

## 1. What AYTHER provides

AYTHER is an independent technology project for applying compatible audiovisual
enhancements to retro games during playback. Its public repositories may provide:

- original source code and build files;
- technical specifications and documentation;
- public APIs, tools, and examples;
- schemas and validation rules for enhancement packs; and
- original project branding subject to the
  [AYTHER Trademark Policy](TRADEMARKS.md).

AYTHER provides technology and interoperability mechanisms. It does not grant
rights in third-party games or content.

## 2. Bring Your Own ROM (BYOR)

AYTHER does not provide, host, sell, bundle, mirror, or distribute commercial
game ROMs.

Users must supply their own game data from a source they are legally permitted
to use. AYTHER does not determine whether a user owns a lawful copy, whether a
backup is authorized, or whether a particular use qualifies for an exception or
limitation under applicable law.

Official AYTHER repositories and releases must not contain:

- commercial ROMs or ROM fragments;
- ROM patches that reproduce protected game content beyond what applicable law
  permits;
- download links, scripts, configuration, or instructions primarily intended
  to obtain unauthorized ROMs;
- encryption keys, access keys, leaked material, or credentials used to obtain
  protected game content; or
- files disguised, split, encoded, encrypted, or renamed to evade these rules.

Hashes, checksums, metadata, or locally derived identifiers may be used for
compatibility and validation when they do not reconstruct or distribute the
underlying game. Matching a hash does not establish ownership or authorization.

## 3. Bring Your Own Core (BYOC)

AYTHER does not claim ownership of third-party emulator cores. Unless an
official release expressly states otherwise, users obtain compatible cores
separately and are responsible for complying with each core's license and any
applicable law.

An AYTHER interface, adapter, or compatibility statement does not:

- relicense a third-party core;
- remove that core's source-distribution, attribution, notice, or copyleft
  obligations;
- represent that the core is endorsed by or affiliated with AYTHER; or
- guarantee that combining or distributing particular components is legally
  permitted.

Contributors must keep third-party components and their licenses identifiable.
Bundling a core into an AYTHER distribution requires a documented license review
and compliance with all applicable obligations.

## 4. BIOS, firmware, keys, and access controls

Official AYTHER repositories and releases must not distribute proprietary BIOS
files, firmware, decryption keys, signing keys, device secrets, or other
restricted platform material without documented authorization.

AYTHER is not intended to defeat digital-rights-management systems, licensing
checks, authentication controls, encryption, or technical protection measures.
Code or instructions whose primary purpose is unauthorized circumvention are
not accepted into official project resources.

Nothing in this section limits security research, interoperability work, reverse
engineering, preservation activity, or other conduct that is independently
permitted by applicable law. Anyone relying on an exception is responsible for
confirming its scope in the relevant jurisdiction.

## 5. Enhancement packs

An AYTHER enhancement pack is separate from the original game. A format,
validator, signature, compatibility identifier, or listing does not establish
that the pack's contents are authorized.

Pack authors and distributors must have the necessary rights to every element
they publish, including:

- artwork, sprites, backgrounds, interfaces, fonts, and icons;
- music, sound recordings, sound effects, voices, and performances;
- video, animation, text, translations, and narrative material;
- logos, names, characters, likenesses, and other brand identifiers;
- code, scripts, models, datasets, and generated content; and
- any material derived from a third-party game or other protected work.

Redrawing, upscaling, vectorizing, filtering, rearranging, separating into tiles,
using generative tools, or otherwise transforming protected content does not
automatically create permission to distribute it. Whether material is original,
licensed, permitted by an exception, or an unauthorized derivative work depends
on the facts and applicable law.

Official AYTHER repositories and distribution channels may reject or remove a
pack when authorization is absent, unclear, misleading, or reasonably disputed.
A technically valid or cryptographically signed pack is not necessarily
approved, licensed, safe, or lawful.

## 6. Provenance, licenses, and attribution

Publicly distributed packs and substantial contributed assets should include
clear provenance and licensing information identifying:

- the author or rights holder;
- the source of each material element;
- the applicable license or written authorization;
- required attribution and notices;
- whether the material was modified;
- relevant tool-generated or third-party components; and
- any restrictions that recipients must observe.

Contributors must not remove authorship, copyright, license, provenance, or
trademark notices they are required to retain. A source being publicly
accessible, free of charge, or downloadable from an official website does not
by itself authorize redistribution or modification.

## 7. Third-party names and trademarks

AYTHER is an independent project. It is not affiliated with, authorized by,
endorsed by, or sponsored by Sega or any other game publisher, developer,
console manufacturer, platform operator, or rights holder.

Third-party names and trademarks may be used only when reasonably necessary to
identify compatibility, report facts, provide commentary, or make another use
permitted by applicable law. Such use must not imply official status,
certification, sponsorship, or endorsement.

Compatibility references should be factual and visually secondary to AYTHER or
the independent project's own branding. Third-party logos, trade dress, and
promotional artwork must not be included merely to make a project appear

The use of AYTHER's own marks is separately governed by
[TRADEMARKS.md](TRADEMARKS.md).

## 8. Contributions to official repositories

By submitting a contribution, the contributor represents that, to the best of
their knowledge:

- they created the contribution or have authority to submit it;
- the contribution may be distributed under the repository's stated license;
- required third-party notices and source information are included;
- the contribution does not contain secrets, credentials, personal data, or
  confidential material they lack permission to disclose; and
- they have not knowingly included unauthorized game content or other material
  prohibited by this policy.

Unless expressly stated otherwise, contributions intentionally submitted for
inclusion in a repository licensed under Apache License 2.0 are handled under
Section 5 of that license. A repository may adopt additional contribution terms
when clearly documented.

Maintainers may request evidence of provenance or authorization, decline a
contribution, remove material, or pause distribution while a rights question is
reviewed.

## 9. No endorsement or legal certification

The following do not constitute legal review, permission from a third-party
rights holder, or endorsement by AYTHER:

- compatibility with an AYTHER API or format;
- successful validation by an AYTHER tool;
- a cryptographic signature;
- inclusion in a community index;
- an issue, pull request, discussion, or link in an AYTHER resource; or
- use of terms such as “compatible with AYTHER.”

Only an express written statement from the relevant rights holder can establish
authorization on that rights holder's behalf.

## 10. Reporting intellectual-property concerns

Rights holders or their authorized representatives may report a concern to
[david.lazarte@gmail.com](mailto:david.lazarte@gmail.com).

A report should include:

1. the reporter's name, organization, and contact information;
2. identification of the copyrighted work, trademark, or other right at issue;
3. the exact URL, repository path, release, or other location of the material;
4. an explanation of why the use is unauthorized or misleading;
5. a statement that the report is made in good faith;
6. confirmation that the reporter is the rights holder or is authorized to act
   for the rights holder; and
7. a physical or electronic signature.

Please do not send confidential evidence unless requested. Knowingly false,
misleading, or abusive reports may have legal consequences.

When content is hosted by GitHub, a rights holder may also use GitHub's
applicable content-removal process. A report to AYTHER does not replace any
formal notice that applicable law or a hosting provider requires.

## 11. Review and response process

After receiving a sufficiently detailed report, project maintainers may:

- acknowledge receipt and request missing information;
- temporarily restrict, unlist, or remove the identified material;
- notify the contributor or distributor responsible for it;
- preserve relevant records;
- request evidence of authorization, provenance, or an applicable legal
  exception;
- restore content when the concern is resolved; or
- refer the parties to GitHub, another service provider, or appropriate legal
  counsel.

AYTHER does not adjudicate ownership disputes. Maintainers may take reasonable,
reversible precautions while the parties resolve a dispute and may comply with
valid legal orders or provider requirements.

## 12. Privacy and personal information

Contributors must not publish personal information, private communications,
credentials, access tokens, private keys, or confidential records without
authorization.

Security vulnerabilities and exposed credentials should not be reported in a
public issue. Follow the private reporting instructions in SECURITY.md when
available or contact [david.lazarte@gmail.com](mailto:david.lazarte@gmail.com).
